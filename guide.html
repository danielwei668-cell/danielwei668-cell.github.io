How to choose a jaw crusher

The right model is decided by four inputs, in this order. Send them to us and we confirm the model and its real specification before quoting — this page explains the logic so you can check our recommendation.

Step 1 — Maximum feed size first

Measure the largest lump you will actually feed. The crusher's maximum feed size must exceed it, or the machine will block. This one number already narrows the PE series: up to 180 mm points to the PE200×300, up to 210 mm to the PE250×400, and larger lumps move you up the specification table.

Step 2 — Material and hardness

Hard granite or gold quartz ore wears jaw plates faster and crushes slower than soft limestone. Tell us the material honestly — it changes both the model choice and which wear parts we include.

Step 3 — Required output size

Each model has an adjustable discharge range (for example 20–70 mm on the PE250×400). If the crushed stone feeds a ball mill, a smaller, even output shortens grinding time.

Step 4 — Target capacity per hour

Match capacity last, not first. A crusher's stated t/h assumes a discharge setting and material; the same PE250×400 rated 5–25 t/h sits at the low end on hard ore with a fine setting. We confirm the realistic figure for your material in writing.

Diesel or electric?

Choose diesel where grid power is unreliable or the site moves — common at small mines and quarries in Africa. Choose electric where stable power exists, for lower running cost. The PE200×300 and PE250×400 are available either way.

Frequently asked questions

How do I choose the right jaw crusher model?

Start from the largest stone or ore lump you will feed: the crusher's maximum feed size must be bigger than it. Then fix the output size you need and your target tonnes per hour, and consider the material's hardness. Those four inputs — material and hardness, feed size, output size, target capacity — determine the PE model; choosing by capacity alone often selects the wrong machine.

Should I choose a diesel or an electric jaw crusher?

Choose diesel where grid power is unreliable or the site moves, which is common at small mines and quarries; choose electric where stable power is available, for lower running cost and simpler maintenance. Boorant's PE200×300 and PE250×400 jaw crushers are available with either an electric motor or a diesel engine.

What is the difference between the PE200×300 and the PE250×400 jaw crusher?

The PE250×400 is one size up: its feed opening is 250×400 mm against 200×300 mm, it accepts feed up to 210 mm against 180 mm, discharges 20–70 mm against 15–50 mm, and produces 5–25 t/h against 3–10 t/h, with a 15 kW motor against 11 kW. Choose the PE250×400 when your feed lumps or target capacity exceed what the PE200×300 can take.

When do I need a ball mill as well as a jaw crusher?

A jaw crusher only breaks ore into small stones; gold recovery usually needs the ore ground to a fine powder, which is the ball mill's job. For gold ore processing, a jaw crusher and a ball mill are normally bought as one line — for example a PE-series jaw crusher feeding a 600×1200 ball mill (0.5–1.2 t/h, wet or dry grinding).
